# Quant Projects

Quant research projects: notebooks, research pages and write-ups.

## Investment Research

| Study | Summary |
|---|---|
| [Pricing the WTI Smile](Investment%20Research/Pricing%20the%20WTI%20Smile/) | Most oil option desks price each strike with its own volatility. That means dozens of models that don't agree with each other. One curve with three numbers fixes this. Refitted each morning, it prices every strike to about 1.3 cents a barrel, where a single flat volatility misses the far strikes by up to 24 cents. Mispriced strikes stand out right away. Its slope even caught the 2026 shift in call skew weeks before the curvature did. |
| [Implied Vol vs. Models Through an Oil Shock](Investment%20Research/Implied%20Vol%20vs%20Models%20Through%20an%20Oil%20Shock/) | Option prices saw the 2026 oil shock coming before any model did. As the shock built, their forecast error was two to nine times smaller. Then they stayed nervous long after the peak. That is where the models took over and where selling volatility paid about twice the usual premium. Switching between the two in real time did not work, because the warning signals fired too often on ordinary days. |
| [Clustering a Multi-Asset Portfolio by Co-Movement](Investment%20Research/Clustering%20a%20Multi-Asset%20Portfolio%20by%20Co-Movement/) | A portfolio of stocks, bonds and commodities can still be one big bet. High-yield bonds, EM bonds and REITs trade much like the equity funds. Take out the market move that drags everything together and 23 ETFs fall into eight clear groups. These match real asset classes twice as well as before. The weak spot is the number of groups, which the algorithm sets anywhere from 2 to 7 depending on the window. |
| [Covariance Denoising for Equity Portfolios](Investment%20Research/Covariance%20Denoising%20for%20Equity%20Portfolios/) | An optimizer treats every estimated correlation as exact. With only six months of data that gets expensive. Removing the noise cut the portfolio's volatility from 21.0% to 13.4% and its trading by about 80%. With two years of data the same filter starts deleting real structure. So the rule is simple. Filter the noise when history is short and use smooth shrinkage when it is long. |
| [Feature Redundancy: Why Individual Importance Can Be Misleading](Investment%20Research/Feature%20Redundancy/) | Quants use feature importance to decide which signals to keep. It fails quietly when features overlap. Add 30 near-copies of five real drivers and the model gives those drivers only 17% of their true credit. Scoring similar features as one group brings them back. On 90 US stocks this shows that past prices predict how big the next move will be. They say nothing about which way it goes. |
| [Nested Clustered Optimization](Investment%20Research/Nested%20Clustered%20Optimization/) | Optimizers treat noisy estimates as if they were true. That makes the portfolios they build fragile. Grouping assets before optimizing makes them much sturdier. In simulation it cut excess risk from 22% to 7% and made the weights three times steadier. Real portfolios from 2017 to 2026 show where this matters most. It pays in books that can short or use leverage, while in long-only books the no-short rule already does most of the work. |
| [Factor Risk Decomposition and Stress Testing](Investment%20Research/Factor%20Risk%20Decomposition%20and%20Stress%20Testing/) | A long/short book with no market exposure looks safe. This one still took 70% of its risk from factor bets. The biggest was a value tilt nobody chose, from being long banks and energy and short technology. A momentum reversal of about two standard deviations would be enough for a 10% monthly loss. Ranking stocks within each sector removed the hidden bet. The worst drawdown fell from 46% to 19%. |
